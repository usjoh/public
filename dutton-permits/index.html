How to read this

  • “Never closed out” means the City’s Application Status is Expired or Active: 62 plus 23. The 9 withdrawn applications are excluded, since a withdrawal is a decision rather than an omission.
  • “Expired, no call for inspection” is the City’s own User Status wording, not an inference. It appears on 44 records.
  • Two status columns. The City keeps an Application Status and a User Status, and they disagree on 20 records that read Complete but are not marked Closed. Both are shown on every row so the difference is visible rather than hidden.
  • Units. 81 records carry no unit in the Location field, mostly parcel-wide and common-area work. They appear under “no unit” rather than being dropped.
Owner names. This page is public, so the names of private individuals have been withheld and appear as Private owner. Company, trust and institutional owners are shown as the City recorded them, since the developer’s filing record is the point of the page. Nothing else is altered: every application, status, date, unit and declared value is exactly as released. The unredacted file is the City’s own public record, available on request from the Division of Development Services.
Two limits worth stating. The list is current as of the City’s 25 August 2026 pull, so anything filed since will not appear. And it records what was filed, not what was inspected: the inspection history behind the 85 unclosed applications has been requested from the City and is not yet in hand. Whether that work was inspected and simply never closed out, or never inspected at all, is not answerable from this data.
